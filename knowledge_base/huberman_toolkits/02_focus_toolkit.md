## Core Idea
Focus is regulated by three neurochemicals: Epinephrine (alertness), Acetylcholine (direction of focus), and Dopamine (sustained attention). Understanding how to manipulate these allows you to control your ability to focus.

---

## The "Arrow Model" of Focus

```
    Epinephrine (arrow shaft — provides energy/alertness)
         ||
         ||  Acetylcholine (arrowhead — directs focus to specific target)
         \/
         []
    Dopamine (fletching — keeps the arrow flying straight, sustains focus over time)
```

- **Epinephrine (Adrenaline)**: Increases energy and alertness, but alone cannot make you focus.
- **Acetylcholine**: Acts like a spotlight, directing your attention to a specific thing.
- **Dopamine**: Sustains motivation and allows focus to persist over time.

Sleep regulates virtually every process in the brain and body, including hormone release, metabolism, and attention. Without good sleep, focus tools will not work effectively.

## Tools to Enhance Focus

### 1. Binaural Beats (40Hz)
- **Why**: 40Hz binaural beats put the brain into a state more conducive to learning and focus.
- **When**: Listen for 5 minutes before starting work or study.
- **Effects**: Proven to increase cognition, relaxation, creativity, reduce pain and anxiety.
- **Apps**: BrainWave (iOS/Android), or free YouTube 40Hz binaural beats audio.

### 2. Caffeine
- **Why**: Caffeine increases dopamine receptors and boosts alertness.
- **Dose**: 1-3mg per kg body weight (e.g., 70kg person: 70-210mg, or ~1-2 coffees).
- **Timing**: 90-120 minutes after waking (to avoid afternoon crash). Or before a focus session.
- **Cycle**: If you're a daily user, consider cycling off every 3-4 months for 2 weeks to reset tolerance.

### 3. Alpha-GPC (Supplement)
- **Why**: Increases acetylcholine availability in the brain.
- **Dose**: 300mg before focus session.
- **Caution**: Some studies link long-term use to stroke risk. Use only on days when you need intense focus.

### 4. L-Tyrosine (Supplement)
- **Why**: Precursor to dopamine. Enhances dopamine transmission.
- **Dose**: 500-1000mg before focus session.
- **Effect**: Noticeable increase in motivation and ability to sustain focus.
- **Caution**: Don't take too late in the day (may disrupt sleep).

### 5. Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA)
- **Why**: Supports mood, cognitive function, and dopamine transmission.
- **Dose**: 1-3g EPA per day.
- **Source**: Fish oil, algae oil.

### 6. Meditation (13-minute daily)
- **Why**: A 2021 study showed that 13 minutes of daily meditation significantly improved focus, mood, and stress levels.
- **Method**: Sit quietly, focus on breathing. When mind wanders, gently return to breath.
- **Timing**: Any time of day, but consistency matters.

## The 90-Minute Ultradian Cycle
- The brain works in ~90-minute cycles called "ultradian rhythms."
- **Optimal focus session**: 90 minutes.
- **First 5-10 minutes**: Hard to focus — this is normal. Push through.
- **Middle 60-70 minutes**: Deep focus.
- **Last 10-15 minutes**: Focus naturally wanes.
- **After**: Take a 10-30 minute break. Do NOT immediately jump into another 90-min block.

### How to structure a focus session
1. **Preparation (5 min)**: Remove phone, close unnecessary tabs, set intention.
2. **Binaural beats (5 min)**: Listen to 40Hz binaural beats.
3. **Work (90 min)**: Single-task. No multi-tasking.
4. **Break (10-30 min)**: Walk, stretch, NSDR, or just relax. No screens.

## Visual Focus & Mental Focus Connection
- **Where your eyes go, your mind follows.**
- Narrowing your visual focus to a specific point (like a screen) increases alertness and concentration.
- Widening your gaze (panoramic vision) lowers stress and relaxes the nervous system.
- **Tip**: When struggling to focus, literally narrow your eyes to a single point on the screen/page for 30-60 seconds. This triggers acetylcholine release.

---

## NSDR for Focus Recovery
- After intense focus sessions, the brain is depleted.
- **NSDR (Non-Sleep Deep Rest)**: 10-30 minute protocol to recharge.
- **Effect**: Restores dopamine levels, reduces fatigue, improves subsequent focus.
- **Free resource**: YouTube "Madefor NSDR" with Andrew Huberman.

---

## Phone & Distraction Management
- Phone presence (even if off) reduces cognitive capacity.
- **Rule**: Phone in another room or in a drawer during focus sessions.
- Notifications fragment attention — each one costs ~15-20 minutes of recovery time.

---

## Time-of-Day Recommendations
- **Morning (first 8h after waking)**: Best for deep, analytical work. Cortisol and alertness peak.
- **Afternoon (8-14h after waking)**: Better for creative work, brainstorming, meetings.
- **Evening (14-24h after waking)**: Light review work only. Brain naturally winds down.
- **Important**: Avoid intense focus sessions within 2-3 hours of bedtime, as they can raise core body temperature and make sleep harder.

---

## Warning Signs of Over-Focus
- If you feel "wired but tired" — you've pushed past the optimal window.
- Solution: Stop, do NSDR, take a walk outside with panoramic vision.
- Chronic over-focus leads to burnout. Balance deep work with deliberate rest.
