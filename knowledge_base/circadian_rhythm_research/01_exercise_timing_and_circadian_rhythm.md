# Exercise Timing, Circadian Rhythm, and Sleep Quality
## Sources
- Circadian Rhythm Exercise and Heart (Chao-Yung Wang, Acta Cardiologica Sinica, 2017)
- Huberman Lab Sleep Toolkit recommendations on exercise timing
- General circadian biology literature

---

## Core Concept: Chronoexercise
The timing of exercise significantly affects its health benefits because the body's physiological systems (heart rate, cortisol, body temperature, metabolism) follow circadian rhythms. Exercising at the right time amplifies benefits; exercising at the wrong time can disrupt sleep and recovery.

---

## Circadian Physiology Timeline

### Cortisol Rhythm
- **Peak**: 7-9 AM (highest)
- **Decline**: Throughout the day, gradually
- **Lowest**: Around midnight to 2 AM
- **Significance**: Cortisol is the "wake up" hormone. Morning exercise synergizes with natural cortisol peak.

### Body Temperature Rhythm
- **Lowest** (Temperature Minimum): ~2 hours before habitual wake time (e.g., 4 AM if waking at 6 AM)
- **Rising**: Throughout the morning
- **Peak**: Late afternoon to early evening (4-7 PM)
- **Declining**: Evening, to prepare for sleep
- **Significance**: Body temperature needs to DROP 1-3°C to initiate sleep. Exercise raises body temperature, which is why late-night exercise can disrupt sleep.

### Melatonin Rhythm
- **Begins rising**: ~2 hours before habitual bedtime (dim light melatonin onset)
- **Peak**: 2-4 AM
- **Suppressed by**: Bright light (especially blue), exercise, food

## Best Times to Exercise by Goal

### 1. Morning (6-9 AM) — Best for:
- Fat loss (body burns more fat when fasted)
- Building the habit (willpower is highest)
- Setting circadian rhythm (synergizes with cortisol peak)
- Blood pressure regulation

**Caveat**: Muscles and joints are stiffer in the morning. Warm up thoroughly.

### 2. Late Afternoon / Early Evening (4-7 PM) — Best for:
- Peak physical performance (body temperature is highest, reaction time fastest)
- Strength training (muscles are warmest)
- Endurance sports
- Injury prevention (joints are most flexible)

### 3. Evening (After 7 PM) — Caution Zone
- Light to moderate exercise (walking, yoga, stretching) is fine and may help sleep.
- **AVOID** intense cardio or heavy lifting within 2-3 hours of bedtime.
- Why: 
  1. Raises core body temperature, which needs to drop for sleep
  2. Stimulates sympathetic nervous system (fight-or-flight)
  3. Boosts cortisol and adrenaline, counteracting melatonin
  4. Increases heart rate, taking hours to return to resting

### 4. Late Night (After 9 PM) — Generally Avoid
- High-intensity exercise this late significantly disrupts sleep architecture.
- If you must: Stick to gentle stretching, walking, or restorative yoga.
- NSDR is a better alternative if you're seeking relaxation.

## How Late Exercise Affects Sleep

### Physiological Mechanisms
1. **Core Temperature**: Exercise raises core temp by 1-2°C. Sleep onset requires temp to DROP. Takes 1-3 hours for temp to normalize after intense exercise.
2. **Heart Rate Variability (HRV)**: Intense exercise lowers HRV (higher stress). Sleep requires high HRV (parasympathetic dominance).
3. **Hormonal Effects**: 
   - Cortisol and norepinephrine spike post-exercise
   - These counteract melatonin and adenosine
4. **Sympathetic Activation**: Exercise activates the "fight or flight" branch. Sleep requires parasympathetic ("rest and digest") dominance.

### Research Findings
- **Morning exercise** (7 AM) improves sleep quality that night (deeper sleep, less waking).
- **Afternoon exercise** (1-4 PM) also benefits sleep, with peak performance gains.
- **Evening vigorous exercise** (within 2h of bed) increases sleep latency (takes longer to fall asleep) and reduces deep sleep percentage.
- **Exception**: Fit individuals who exercise regularly may tolerate evening exercise better than sedentary individuals.

## Recommended Protocol for Your App

### When user has a workout habit but it's already evening:

**Scenario A: Before 7 PM, bedtime at 23:00**
- Workout is fine. Complete by 20:30 to allow 2.5 hours for body temp to normalize.
- Recommend a cool shower afterward to accelerate temperature drop.

**Scenario B: 7-9 PM, bedtime at 23:00**
- Switch to light exercise only: stretching, yoga, walking.
- Or reschedule the workout to next morning.
- If skipping: Note that one missed day is better than disrupted sleep.

**Scenario C: After 9 PM, bedtime at 23:00**
- Skip the workout. Sleep is more important.
- Alternative: Do 15 minutes of stretching or NSDR.
- Reschedule to tomorrow morning.

**Scenario D: Afternoon (before 5 PM), bedtime at 23:00**
- Perfect time for workout. No concerns.
- This is actually the biological peak for physical performance.

---

## Special Cases

### Shift Workers
- Exercise before the night shift starts, not after.
- Use bright lights during exercise to stay alert.
- After shift, use dim lights and gentle stretching.

### Jet Lag Recovery
- Exercise at the destination's morning time to help reset the clock.
- Avoid exercise at times that correspond to nighttime in your origin timezone.

### Hypertension / Cardiovascular Conditions
- Morning blood pressure is highest. If hypertensive, exercise in the afternoon instead.
- Consult a physician for personalized timing.

---

## Key Takeaways for Habit Planning
1. **Morning workouts** (6-9 AM) are biologically ideal for circadian alignment.
2. **Late afternoon** (4-7 PM) is ideal for performance.
3. **Avoid intense exercise within 2-3 hours of bedtime.**
4. **Body temperature** is the key variable — if you can't cool down, you can't sleep.
5. **One missed workout is better than one bad night of sleep.**
6. **Stretching, yoga, and walking** are safe evening alternatives to intense exercise.
